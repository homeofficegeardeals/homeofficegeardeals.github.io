What actually decides it

Marketing copy hides the trade-offs in the same three places on almost every component. These are the ones worth checking against your own setup before you order.

  • Airflow path: the first number that can rule the product out. Measure it against your own setup rather than the photos, because most returns start here.
  • Power supply headroom: where cheaper versions usually cut corners. It is rarely in the headline spec.
  • Cooler clearance: the compatibility detail that is easy to miss on the listing and expensive to discover afterwards.
  • Fan noise: the part that decides the cost over a year, not on day one. Consumables and replacement parts matter more than the sticker.

What the listing leaves out

Product pages rarely mention return shipping, the exact size with packaging, or which parts the warranty excludes. For a component, the three omissions that matter most are airflow path, power supply headroom and fan noise. Check the specification table and the seller's returns page before ordering, not after.

Plan the cable runs before the motherboard goes in. Test the fans on a bench header first.

Who should skip it: A build whose real limit is the graphics card or the monitor, not airflow.

Three mistakes to avoid

  • Buying on the headline figure instead of checking airflow path first.
  • Assuming cooler clearance without reading the specification table.
  • Ordering before checking fan noise and the seller's return terms.
