What actually decides it

Marketing copy hides the trade-offs in the same three places on almost every chair. These are the ones worth checking against your own setup before you order.

  • Lumbar support that adjusts up and down: the first number that can rule the product out. Measure it against your own setup rather than the photos, because most returns start here.
  • Seat depth against your leg length: where cheaper versions usually cut corners. It is rarely in the headline spec.
  • Armrests that adjust in height and width: the compatibility detail that is easy to miss on the listing and expensive to discover afterwards.
  • Weight rating and frame warranty: the part that decides the cost over a year, not on day one. Consumables and replacement parts matter more than the sticker.

What the listing leaves out

Product pages rarely mention return shipping, the exact size with packaging, or which parts the warranty excludes. For a chair, the three omissions that matter most are lumbar support that adjusts up and down, seat depth against your leg length and weight rating and frame warranty. Check the specification table and the seller's returns page before ordering, not after.

Frequently asked questions

How long should assembly take?

Most chairs assemble in 20 to 45 minutes with the included tools. Clear a flat space first.

Do I need a headrest?

Only if you recline for long stretches. For upright work, lumbar support matters more.

What does the warranty usually cover?

Frames and mechanisms more than upholstery. Check whether labor and shipping are included.
